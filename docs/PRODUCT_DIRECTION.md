# Qahwa & Majlis: product direction

## The promise

Build a warm Saudi hospitality game where players merge to prepare, personalize, welcome and remember. Coffee, guests, family and regional details should affect the decisions a player makes, not only the decoration. The owner selected Saudi Arabia's iPhone Casual top-grossing chart as the first top-ten target. The measurement period and a verifiable chart source remain to be chosen. A top-ten position is an aspiration, not a result that can be established from source code; it requires a released game, a viable revenue model and independently verified ranking evidence.

## Ideas to prototype

1. **A complete gathering:** Faisal's tasting now shows the prepared trays and chosen corner together; after the gathering, players can tap Faisal and Noura to revisit their reactions in the tasting and the Majlis. Test whether players notice the interaction and understand the sequence without instructions. The occasion is untimed and replayable without a second reward.
2. **Guests with continuity:** Faisal and Noura each remember the player's selected tray the first time they are served after the tasting. Their saved memory appears in later order guides; tap either guest in the Majlis coffee corner to revisit their gathering reaction and memory. Playtest whether players notice and care about this continuity before extending it to other guests.
3. **Regional hospitality stories:** The Taif chapter leads to an optional rose welcome in the Majlis. It asks the player to prepare a separate scent and refreshment; the saved card shows that choice, and Noura recalls both it and the chapter's drink choice. The harvest detail draws on [Visit Saudi's rose-water guide](https://www.visitsaudi.com/en/taif/stories/rosewater-in-saudi-arabia) and [Saudipedia's distillation overview](https://saudipedia.com/en/how-is-taif-rose-water-made). Have Saudi cultural and dialect reviewers validate the writing and visual references.
4. **Order planning:** The order guide shows whether each item is on the board, in the pantry or still missing, gives the preceding merge or power-boost hint, and can return needed pantry items or spare preceding-tier pieces directly when the board has space. The displayed coin reward includes restoration, area and seasonal bonuses, with a breakdown in the guide. Focus mode highlights relevant generators and lower-level items. The tasting and Taif recipes use the same ingredient guidance. Test discoverability and whether players can follow the path without additional instructions.
5. **Satisfying board recovery:** An immediate undo for an accidental sale remains available through unrelated board and balance changes while the sold slot stays untouched and the proceeds can be returned. Test its discoverability on a phone and observe other sources of frustration, including board congestion and accidental merges.
6. **A personal majlis:** Restored wings show their chosen colors as small architectural and hospitality details beneath the painted scene. Players can tap a restored detail to read a family comment that refers to that wing and its current style. Selecting a style never spends restoration stars; planned styles are free, while changing an already restored wing uses the displayed coin cost. Test whether these interactions make choices feel meaningful and where guests should visibly use the space next.

## Validate before scaling

### Competitive observations and experiments (28 October 2026)

- The Saudi App Store descriptions for [Gossip Harbor](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/gossip-harbor-merge-story/id1623318294), [Merge Mansion](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/merge-mansion-puzzles-story/id1484442152), and [Travel Town](https://apps.apple.com/sa/app/travel-town-merge-adventure/id1521236603) combine merging with customer or character stories and restoring places. Their listed language metadata did not include Arabic when checked; that metadata alone does not establish every language or dialect supported inside the apps. Their listings and individual reviews are not representative player research or evidence of our chart position.
- **Hypothesis: the guest remembers the welcome.** After a player prepares a gathering, make the consequence visible at the next relevant visit: the guest recognizes the chosen tray and the coffee corner shows that gathering's details. The existing tasting, guest memory and corner already provide an initial version. In unprompted playtests, observe whether players notice the connection, can name their earlier choice, and want to revisit the guest; compare their comments before adding more relationships.
- **Hypothesis: an occasion beats another isolated order.** Prototype one short optional, untimed welcome in which two related orders lead to a shared scene in the player's chosen Majlis. Reuse existing items and rewards, avoid extra board clutter, and let the player resume later. Test whether participants understand why the two orders belong together and whether the payoff is worth the steps. The current Faisal tasting is a baseline; do not assume another event is needed until its results are observed.
- **Hypothesis: respectful Arabic-first guidance is a differentiator.** Have Saudi Arabic reviewers assess the existing guest names, order instructions and regional host stories in context, then test comprehension with both new and experienced merge players. Measure misunderstandings and unprompted preference rather than assuming the listings' language metadata proves an unmet need.
- **Guard against friction:** Some individual reviews on the Merge Mansion and Travel Town listings describe energy waiting; another Travel Town review praises its pacing. These are anecdotal and conflicting. Use them as prompts to observe actual waiting and voluntary return behavior in our game, not as evidence that any competitor or our own economy is balanced. The existing simulator cannot predict human enjoyment.

- Run moderated first-session playtests with Arabic-speaking Saudi players on real iPhones, including small and large screens. Record where players hesitate, whether they understand the tasting, and what they remember afterward.
- Test the full native lifecycle: install, first launch, offline play, background/resume, save restoration, orientation, safe areas, audio and haptics. The generated Capacitor iOS project still needs a macOS/Xcode build and device testing.
- Measure session completion, return visits and abandonment at each step of the first gathering with privacy-conscious instrumentation and player consent where applicable. Establish baselines before choosing targets.
- Compare progression pacing and enjoyment with human sessions; the existing economy simulator is useful for finding outliers but cannot establish fun or cultural authenticity.
- **Pacing investigation (three deterministic bot runs, no breaks or gem spending):** In the reward-claiming scenario, the bot averaged 47 merges and 66 generator spawns in Diriyah, rising to 1,117 merges and 1,198 spawns in Al-Khobar. The latter took 60–317 simulated minutes, with energy waits in some runs. Without optional claims, later areas waited substantially longer. These are model outputs, not predicted human times: the bot repeatedly taps and merges, buys energy with coins when it can, and skips story play. Ask testers to mark when goals become repetitive, whether they use boosts and rewards, and whether the next story or visual change feels worth the effort. Compare actions per completed wing and voluntary return sessions before tuning order levels, star costs or energy rewards.
- **Boost comparison:** With the same three seeds and rewards, an adaptive generator boost reduced Al-Khobar's average to 393 merges and 475 spawns, but accumulated 211 minutes of simulated energy waiting on average; the fixed ×1 bot averaged 88 minutes there. Across the preceding Riyadh area, the boosted bot averaged 411 minutes of waiting versus none for the fixed-boost bot. This heuristic picks a boost from currently missing order items, buys coin energy when affordable and does not model real breaks or the player's full set of choices. Its decisions and random paths differ between scenarios, so these numbers identify a tradeoff to investigate, not a causal estimate or balance target. In playtests, watch whether players understand the boost's energy cost and when they change it.
- Delay social competition and additional timed reward tracks until the single-player loop earns repeat play and the team can support live operations.
- The current Majlis showcase uses fictional local examples, not online players; choices stay on the device. Real-money purchase buttons remain unavailable until a verified purchase and restore flow exists.
