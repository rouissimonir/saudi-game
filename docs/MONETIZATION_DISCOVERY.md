# Monetization discovery: Qahwa & Majlis

Status: Test Store preparation, not a functioning store. The owner's first target is Saudi Arabia's iPhone Casual top-grossing chart, and they want to explore a mix of customization and convenience purchases. A chart result depends on release, adoption and revenue; initial Saudi prices exist for draft App Store products, but a revenue target and ranking are not established here.

## Principles and hypotheses

- Keep the first gathering, guest relationships, regional story and board recovery available without payment. Test whether players enjoy the core loop before adjusting its pacing to sell relief.
- Prototype a permanent, optional Majlis decoration collection as a non-consumable: the player can see what it changes before choosing to buy. Test perceived value and cultural fit with Saudi players before offering it for sale.
- Consider a convenience bundle only after observing real energy waits and testing fair free progression. Make contents and localized price clear; avoid randomized paid rewards in the initial offer. These are hypotheses, not approved products.
- Do not assume that the existing local `vipPassActive` save field proves a purchase. It changes generator odds and can be transferred with the save; an eventual paid entitlement must come from verified store state and cannot trust a player-editable save alone. Decide separately how to handle existing local saves without taking away legitimate access.

## Current implementation and decisions

### First offer concepts to validate

These are design proposals, not live benefits or promised prices. Show them to Saudi players and compare willingness to pay, perceived fairness and return behavior before deciding what to build or sell.

| Offer | Proposed player experience | Open questions and delivery needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Majlis membership — monthly** | An optional members' corner style while membership is active, plus a fixed energy credit automatically delivered for each paid subscription period, including periods when the player does not open the game. The original styles, story and guests stay free. Show the billing period and renewal terms clearly; if membership ends, return the corner to the player's last free style. Purchased credits do not expire. | Test whether a temporary style and recurring bonus justify a subscription. Set the bonus after observing energy waits; revisit the draft price based on player feedback. Ownership must follow the active store entitlement. Credits require durable, idempotent delivery for every eligible paid period and reconciliation after absence, renewal, refund or account change; do not depend on daily check-ins. Purchased credits must be preserved even when the normal energy meter is full, for example in a separate balance. |

The Test Store calls this the starter pack, but it is currently configured as repeatable. | As a comparison, the existing free daily shop gift gives 40 energy, and the existing in-game exchange gives 50 energy for 20 gems. Use those anchors to test bundle value; the amount is undecided and the draft price needs player validation. A durable transaction and balance ledger is required before selling it. |
| **Majlis decor collection — permanent** | A visible set of optional corner details inspired by the game's hospitality theme, previewable before purchase and usable after restoration. | Art and cultural review are needed. Verify ownership and restore across devices; no gameplay progression should depend on owning it. |

### Preparing an isolated test bundle

Set `VITE_REVENUECAT_TEST_KEY` in the shell to the project's **public Test Store key** from RevenueCat, then run `npm run build:revenuecat-test`. The command refuses non-test keys and writes to ignored `build/revenuecat-test`, leaving the normal `dist` release bundle untouched. Do not commit the test bundle or submit it to Apple. This command produces web assets only; it does not install or run the native app.

For a native test, use a disposable checkout on a Mac: temporarily set Capacitor's `webDir` to `build/revenuecat-test`, run `npx cap sync ios`, and build a debug simulator or signed development app in Xcode. Confirm the development-only panel appears in the shop. Exercise load, cancellation, successful monthly and decor test purchases, errors and user-initiated restore; compare active entitlements with the RevenueCat Test Store customer record. The panel grants no in-game benefits and does not purchase the consumable. Record the results and device/build. Restore the source config afterward; before any release, rebuild with `npm run build`, sync from `dist`, audit the bundle, and verify that no Test Store key or panel is present. A signed build requires the owner's Apple team and registered identifier. RevenueCat Test Store does not prove Apple Sandbox or real App Store behavior.

The `RevenueCat Test Store simulator` GitHub Actions workflow builds this synthetic test bundle in an ephemeral macOS checkout and attempts simulator installation, launch and an automated read of the three test packages. It uses the project's public Test Store key, never exports a signed app, and uploads only a launch screenshot. The probe requires a success marker in the simulator console; a missing marker may also reflect console capture or startup failure. This new probe has not yet passed in CI. Even a successful probe does not exercise purchases, restore, fulfillment or a physical device. Check the run and inspect the screenshot before describing results; use the signed-device procedure above to exercise transactions.

## Required before enabling a real offer

1. Define the product, ownership duration, price source and benefits; review Arabic and English copy and confirm the core loop remains enjoyable without purchase. Use store-provided localized prices rather than hardcoded amounts.
2. Configure the App Store product and signing/distribution environment. For a restorable product, provide a visible restore action and reconcile verified ownership, including revocation and account changes. See Apple's [purchase and restore documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/offering-completing-and-restoring-in-app-purchases/).
3. Persist delivery safely and reconcile transaction IDs idempotently before finishing transactions. Do not allow repeated delivery on retries, relaunch, restores, transfer-code import or multiple devices. Consumables require a separate durable balance and reconciliation design; a restorable entitlement is not a consumable balance. Apple's [consumable history documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/BundleResources/Information-Property-List/SKIncludeConsumableInAppPurchaseHistory) warns against relying only on device state for reconciliation.
4. Handle cancellation, deferred approval, offline or unavailable products, payment failure, interrupted delivery, revocation and restore failure without falsely granting or losing content. Provide a clear support path and no placeholder paid buttons.
5. Validate in StoreKit testing and the Apple sandbox, then on a signed physical iPhone: purchase, cancel, restore, reinstall, relaunch, background during purchase and retry delivery. Record product type, environment, build, device and observed result. A simulator compile or web preview cannot validate a real purchase.
6. Before release, recheck the applicable [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), including digital purchases, non-expiring purchased currency and disclosure of paid randomized-item odds where relevant. Review privacy, security, support and regional pricing with the responsible team.

After launch, evaluate the chosen Saudi Casual grossing chart with a documented source and measurement period, alongside player retention, purchase conversion, refunds and reliability. Do not interpret a single chart position or a small playtest as proof of a durable top-10 game.
